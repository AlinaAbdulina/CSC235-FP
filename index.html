Visual Analytics for Environmental Data

Who is in Paradise Pond?

An interactive exploration of upstream and downstream environmental patterns in and around Paradise Pond and the Mill River.

This project explores how Paradise Pond may shape river conditions and aquatic life. We compare conditions before and after sediment redistribution and examine how macroinvertebrate feeding groups relate to changes in pH, conductivity, and flow.

About the Project

What are we exploring?

We are studying how sediment redistribution in Paradise Pond may be connected to changes in the Mill River ecosystem. The dashboard compares upstream and downstream sampling sites and lets users inspect how environmental variables relate to macroinvertebrate feeding groups.

The views are linked, so selecting one feeding group updates all charts at once. This helps users move between broad patterns and more focused group-level comparisons.

Study frame

Seasons sampled: Summer and Fall

Before period: 2019

After period: 2020–2024

Site comparison

Upstream = control

Downstream = impact

Why upstream and downstream?

Upstream gives us a control site, while downstream helps us look for possible effects connected to the pond and sediment redistribution.

Why these variables?

We focus on pH, conductivity, and flow because these variables help tell the environmental story more clearly than water temperature for this question.

What can users do?

Users can compare feeding groups across upstream and downstream locations, filter by season and period, and inspect individual points in the charts.

Research Context

What we have learned so far

Season matters

Summer tends to be more stable, while Fall is more variable, so season needs to stay visible in the interface.

Downstream often differs

Downstream generally shows lower pH and slightly higher flow and conductivity, along with greater overall macroinvertebrate density.

Groups respond differently

Collector filterers are often more abundant downstream, while scraper and collector gatherer patterns are more mixed and dynamic.
